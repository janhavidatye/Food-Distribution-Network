# Demand Planning & Inventory Control System Design – Food Distribution Network

## Project Overview
Designed an end-to-end seasonal production and distribution system for a holiday food distribution network, projecting **7,200 units across a 23-day holiday window**. The project integrates demand forecasting, aggregate planning, Lean manufacturing principles, vendor selection, risk management, and micro-distribution planning into a single cohesive supply chain system.

---

## Objectives
- Develop a top-down demand forecast and translate it into a workable production schedule
- Apply Lean tools to reduce waste and optimize throughput on a small-scale production line
- Build a vendor selection framework for a 6-category Bill of Materials (BOM)
- Identify and mitigate supply chain risks using a structured risk matrix
- Design a micro-distribution plan ensuring cold-chain integrity and short lead times

## Key Results
- Achieved **~95% on-time delivery** through hybrid aggregate planning (~130 packs/week) and daily production scheduling
- Balanced a 2-worker production line to **20–22 packs/day** with capacity utilization below 90% using Lean line balancing
- Reduced production waste and improved throughput by implementing **5S, Kanban, and SMED**
- Built a **6-category vendor selection framework** evaluated on USDA/HACCP certifications, lead time, cost, and sustainability — recommending a Texas-based supplier network with backup vendors
- Identified and mitigated **12 supply chain risks** using a P×I matrix
- Designed a micro-distribution plan achieving **2–3 day lead times** with cold-chain integrity
- Projected **~18.8% net profit margin** on $15,840 seasonal revenue

## Methodology

### 1. Demand Forecasting
Applied a **top-down forecasting approach**, breaking projected total demand of 7,200 units into weekly and daily targets. Accounted for seasonal demand spikes across the 23-day holiday window.

### 2. Aggregate Planning
Developed a **hybrid aggregate planning strategy** balancing production rates (~130 packs/week), workforce levels, and inventory buffers to meet demand while keeping capacity utilization below 90%.

### 3. Lean Implementation
- **5S:** Organized workspace to reduce search time and improve flow
- **Kanban:** Implemented pull-based replenishment to control WIP and prevent overproduction
- **SMED:** Reduced setup/changeover time between production runs
- **Line Balancing:** Distributed tasks across 2 workers to minimize idle time and achieve 20–22 packs/day

### 4. Vendor Selection
Built a **weighted multi-criteria scoring model** across 6 BOM categories, evaluating vendors on:
- USDA/HACCP certifications
- Lead time reliability
- Unit cost and total landed cost
- Sustainability practices
- Geographic proximity (Texas-based preferred)

### 5. Risk Management
Identified **12 supply chain risks** across procurement, production, and distribution. Each risk scored using a **P×I matrix** (Probability × Impact) with defined mitigation and contingency strategies.

### 6. Distribution Planning
Designed a micro-distribution plan achieving **2–3 day lead times** while maintaining cold-chain integrity, including temperature-controlled routing and delivery zone mapping.
